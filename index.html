Real first aid for real adventure.

This course is built for people who actually go outside — not just people checking a certification box. Whether your crew is chasing waves, flying sites, riding horses, skiing backcountry lines, paddling rivers, climbing rock, camping off-grid, leading trips, or traveling where emergency response may be delayed, Wilderness First Aid gives you the confidence to assess, stabilize, and make smart decisions under pressure.

For Adventure Communities

Ideal for surf crews, boaters, paddlers, paragliders, climbers, skiers, snowboarders, horseback riders, mountain bikers, hikers, overlanders, camp staff, guides, retreat leaders, and adventure-based businesses.

Scenario-Based Training

Learn through practical emergencies, outdoor problem solving, patient assessment, evacuation thinking, and decision-making when conditions are not perfect.

Built for Remote Settings

Focused on situations where advanced medical care may be delayed, unavailable, or difficult to access due to terrain, water, weather, or travel.

Adventure spaces we train for

From beaches to backcountry, rivers to ridgelines, ranches to remote roads — the course can be tailored to the actual risks your group faces.

Surfing & Coastal

Beach injuries, marine environments, heat, wounds, water response awareness, and travel planning.

Boating & Paddling

Remote water access, delayed rescue, weather exposure, river trips, sailing, kayaking, and rafting.

Skiing & Snow Sports

Cold exposure, hypothermia, fractures, evacuation choices, and winter backcountry emergencies.

Horseback & Ranch

Falls, remote property incidents, ranch crews, trail riding, hunting groups, and rural response planning.

Hiking & Backpacking

Trail injuries, dehydration, exposure, navigation issues, group leadership, and emergency communication.

Climbing & Mountain Sports

Falls, trauma, head injuries, altitude concerns, splinting, monitoring, and evacuation decision-making.

Paragliding & Air Sports

Landing zone incidents, trauma care, scene coordination, remote access, and emergency planning.

Overlanding & Travel

International trips, vehicle-based adventure, remote roads, group travel, and delayed medical care.

What the course covers

Emergency Assessment

Scene safety, patient assessment, life threats, communication, and priorities of care.

Trauma & Injury Care

Bleeding control, wounds, fractures, sprains, burns, head injuries, and spinal concerns.

Environmental Emergencies

Heat illness, hypothermia, dehydration, altitude concerns, bites, stings, and exposure.

Evacuation Decisions

When to stay, when to move, how to monitor a patient, and how to communicate with rescuers.
